# Five guided paper studies

These studies show how to move from the [course notebooks](readme.md) to research papers. Each is designed for one 90-minute discussion plus two hours of independent work. Read the source articles themselves; the questions deliberately direct you to their figures and methods rather than substitute for them. Use the [paper atlas](paper_atlas.md) for the rest of the lab's cryo-related publications.

## Study 1 — How can an unknown structure be found in a cell?

**Papers:** [template-free detection (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btr207), [Multi-Pattern Pursuit (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2019.01.005), [DISCA (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2213149120). **Prepare with:** notebooks 07, 08, 12, and 16.

Begin with the biological ambition: recover the shapes, locations, abundance, and possibly identities of recurring assemblies inside cells without asking in advance for one known structure. A template search is powerful if the target is known, but it does not discover a novel shape by itself. The 2011 paper turns this into a detection problem. In 2019, Multi-Pattern Pursuit (MPP) starts from heterogeneous picked subtomograms and seeks recurring aligned structural patterns; its output includes pattern shapes and their spatial information. In 2023, DISCA uses an iterative deep clustering approach to sort large heterogeneous subtomogram sets into relatively homogeneous groups before downstream alignment and averaging. The 2023 paper explicitly presents that sorting role as complementary to high-resolution refinement rather than claiming the clustering step itself is a high-resolution reconstruction.

**Read and draw.** For each paper, draw the input and output with dimensions: a tomogram, candidate coordinates, a set of subtomograms, clusters, class averages, and re-embedded locations. Mark where particle picking happens. A false negative at picking cannot be recovered by a clustering algorithm that only sees picked crops. Mark where pose is estimated, and where the manuscript provides biological evidence for interpreting a structural pattern. Compare the computational bottleneck each paper treats: detection, heterogeneity and alignment, or throughput of sorting. Write one sentence beginning “The new paper changes ___ while retaining ___.”

**Mini-experiment.** In notebook 12, add a nuisance feature to the two-dimensional feature vectors. Make its scale five times the biological feature. Re-run clustering and report whether groups follow the nuisance. Then normalize or remove that feature and repeat. This models why unsupervised groups must be tested for correlation with tomogram, acquisition condition, orientation, or image quality. It does not simulate the full MPP or DISCA algorithms.

**Evidence check.** For the 2023 paper, identify an experimental dataset and the independent evidence used to interpret one recovered pattern. List what is measured directly and what is inferred. A named molecular complex requires more than a colorful cluster plot. Finish with a rare-structure question: how might a method optimized for representative abundant patterns miss a rare complex, and what experiment would reveal that miss?

## Study 2 — Why does orientation remain hard after picking?

**Papers:** [Fourier-space constrained matching (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsb.2012.02.014), [fast alignment plus maximum likelihood (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/bty267), [Gum-Net (2020)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Zeng_Gum-Net_Unsupervised_Geometric_Matching_for_Fast_and_Accurate_3D_Subtomogram_CVPR_2020_paper.html), [BOE-ViT (2025)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Jiang_BOE-ViT_Boosting_Orientation_Estimation_with_Equivariance_in_Self-Supervised_3D_Subtomogram_CVPR_2025_paper.html). **Prepare with:** notebooks 05, 06, 08, and 15.

Alignment asks for a rotation and translation that bring corresponding molecular density into register. In clean 2D toy data, notebook 06 can search a small set of rotations and shifts. Real subtomograms are 3D, crowded, noisy, and affected by missing Fourier information. There may be symmetry-equivalent poses. A correct match should lead to a more coherent independent average and stable structural features, not merely a high score against a reference.

The 2012 paper emphasizes fast volumetric matching with Fourier-space constraints. The 2018 work combines a fast alignment stage with maximum-likelihood refinement and classification. Gum-Net investigates unsupervised geometric matching. BOE-ViT uses self-supervision from geometric transformations and adds mechanisms intended to improve shift and rotation equivariance in a 3D vision-transformer model. Read each methods diagram and fill four columns: **pose representation**, **training signal**, **search/refinement strategy**, and **validation metric**. Do not infer that “learned” automatically means more accurate or that “fast” means the same computation was solved exactly.

**Mini-experiment.** Add noise to notebook 06's rotated pattern, then repeat its search at 20 random seeds. Record the fraction of correct transforms. Next replace the pattern with a symmetric cross. Several rotations become indistinguishable. Explain why an orientation metric needs to account for symmetry, and why averaging might still be useful when the exact pose label is ambiguous.

**Evidence check.** For BOE-ViT, find the experiment that changes noise level and the evaluation of rotation and translation errors. Ask whether the reported improvement is on simulated ground-truth poses, experimental averages, or both. A pose error measured on simulations and a map-resolution improvement measured on real particles support different conclusions. Propose a test on a new specimen type, with the complete picking and alignment pipeline fixed before looking at the test results.

## Study 3 — How much annotation is enough for a biological claim?

**Papers:** [few-shot classification (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008227), [active learning (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btab123), [CryoSAM (2024)](https://papers.miccai.org/miccai-2024/178-Paper0532.html), [CryoMAE (2025)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2025/papers/Xu_CryoMAE_Few-Shot_Cryo-EM_Particle_Picking_with_Masked_Autoencoders_WACV_2025_paper.pdf), and the [2026 TopoFuse entry](https://xulabs.github.io/publication/). **Prepare with:** notebooks 07, 09, 10, 11, 19, and 20.

The papers here solve related but distinct tasks. Few-shot classification assigns an identity to a crop given very few labeled examples. Active learning selects examples for an expert to label. CryoMAE addresses particle picking in cryo-EM micrographs with few examples and masked-autoencoder features. CryoSAM uses a prompt and a pre-existing 2D foundation model to segment a 3D tomogram; the paper describes cross-plane self-prompting and hierarchical feature matching so one particle-specific prompt can support further instances of that category. TopoFuse, as listed by the lab, targets topology-aware tri-planar segmentation. Do not compare their scalar scores as if they used the same target, annotation unit, or dataset.

**Make an annotation ledger.** For each paper, write how many labels or prompts were supplied, whether they were particle centers, classes, 2D masks, or 3D masks, and whether pretraining used other labeled data. Then state the actual held-out unit: image, tomogram, specimen, or acquisition domain. “Training-free” in the target task does not mean the foundation model was never trained. Ask how a new protein class or instrument changes the task and the annotation budget.

**Mini-experiment.** In notebook 07, choose one threshold that prioritizes recall for discovery and one that prioritizes precision for averaging. In notebook 09, create a one-voxel break across a thin mask and compare Dice before and after. If Dice changes little but connectivity changes, describe why a topology-aware method might matter biologically. Avoid treating that toy mask as evidence about a named paper's performance.

**Evidence check.** Select one category in CryoSAM's full-tomogram result and trace a prompt to the instance masks and then to semantic coverage. Identify one likely propagation error and one independent manual audit. For a newer TopoFuse manuscript, wait for the full paper before asserting which topological measure improved; until then, the title defines a reading question, not a verified result.

## Study 4 — When does a cleaner image become less trustworthy?

**Papers:** [crowded-tomogram simulation (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-016-1283-3), [CryoETGAN (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2022.760404), [DUAL (2024 preprint)](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.03.02.583135), the [2025 J-Invariant Volume Shuffle entry](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vQOw1_7_1a6HuyyvcwmtOzfliOmyz4L7giYkp3-i0MOxh-ASU0ZCjNtdYZZ4KC3UlFHdC9Gb-gzXMsi/pub), and the [2026 score-based cryo-EM denoising entry](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vQOw1_7_1a6HuyyvcwmtOzfliOmyz4L7giYkp3-i0MOxh-ASU0ZCjNtdYZZ4KC3UlFHdC9Gb-gzXMsi/pub). **Prepare with:** notebooks 03, 04, 13, 14, and 20.

A simulator can provide known ground truth for testing pickers, but its realism is limited by its assumptions. A denoiser can improve visibility, but it can also remove a weak real feature or insert a plausible feature. The two concerns meet when synthetic examples train or evaluate a restoration model. Record the target of each paper: making images for evaluation, translating image appearance, jointly learning simulation and denoising, learning from one noisy volume, or imposing a score-based prior on noisy cryo-EM data. These targets are not interchangeable.

**Build a provenance chain.** For each dataset, note which images are experimental and which are generated, how ground truth was obtained, and whether the test includes real held-out specimens. Identify at least three real-world changes a simulator should vary: crowding, ice thickness, dose, defocus, or the missing wedge. Ask whether a model could exploit a simulator artifact that correlates with class labels. If a paper reports a better downstream metric after denoising, ask whether the downstream method was tuned equally for the raw and processed inputs.

**Mini-experiment.** In notebook 14, make the signal peak one sample wide. The neighbor-only predictor cannot see that peak at its center and may erase it. In notebook 20, increase the prior weight until a feature absent from the measurement appears. Write two separate failure descriptions: loss of a rare true structure and invention of a plausible false one.

**Evidence check.** Design a blinded inspection in which an analyst sees raw, denoised, and independently averaged views in randomized order. Define one quantitative preservation metric, one hallucination check, and one biological endpoint. A visually attractive denoised image is a useful hypothesis generator; it is not by itself evidence for a new molecular assembly.

## Study 5 — Cryo-EM images, maps, and biological interpretation

**Papers:** [component fitting into cryo-EM maps (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btq201), [resolution-guided homogeneous image selection (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40484-019-0191-8), [CryoMAE (2025)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2025/papers/Xu_CryoMAE_Few-Shot_Cryo-EM_Particle_Picking_with_Masked_Autoencoders_WACV_2025_paper.pdf), and the [fungal plasma membrane study (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-64171-x). **Prepare with:** notebooks 01–04, 08, 16, and 17.

This study connects the cryo-EM branch to a biological application. Particle picking selects candidate images; quality or homogeneity selection changes which particles contribute to a reconstruction; fitting places candidate components into a map; and a cell or membrane study interprets structures in biological context. Every step changes what uncertainty remains. A 3D map can show shape but may not uniquely establish subunit composition. A fitted atomic model inherits uncertainty from both the map and the candidate structure. Cellular context can support biological meaning but also increases crowding and ambiguity.

**Draw the evidence ladder.** Write one line for each of: specimen, raw images, selected particles, independent half maps, fitted model, biological claim. For the 2020 selection paper, identify the selection objective and check whether resolution was assessed on data independent of the selection process. For the 2010 fitting paper, identify the score used to place components and one alternative arrangement to test. For CryoMAE, distinguish picking quality from final map quality. For the fungal membrane study, trace one proposed antifungal mechanism through imaging observations and any independent experimental evidence.

**Mini-experiment.** Use notebook 17's orthogonal projections to demonstrate an ambiguity that survives one view and is reduced by more views. Use notebook 16's two candidate fits to show that a lower residual favors one proposal under the toy model but does not prove molecular identity. Add a third candidate with more adjustable parameters. Discuss why flexible fitting can overfit weak density.

**Final synthesis.** Choose one early and one recent paper and make a 6-panel presentation: biological question; measured data; inverse problem; method; independent evidence; next falsifying experiment. The final slide must state one inference the data cannot yet support. This is the standard needed to move from knowing the vocabulary to reading the research critically.
